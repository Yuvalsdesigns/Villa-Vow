Notes to each other

Decide these in order

1

Set your budget and a rough guest count

Everything after this scales off these two numbers, venue size, catering cost, even which regions are realistic. Get these roughly right before you fall for a specific venue, not after.

2

Shortlist regions and venues, kosher catering + rabbi first

Kosher catering in Europe is done by a small number of traveling companies and needs the longest lead time of anything on this list: some ask for 12–18 months notice. Confirm a kosher caterer and your officiating rabbi can both work at a venue before you put down any deposit. Look for a villa or masseria with enough bedrooms for your closest guests, plus a walkable cluster of overflow hotels or Airbnbs, so nobody needs a car to get to the pool party. The shortlist covers hidden Italian lakes, Montenegro, Istria, Provence hamlets, Cilento, Lake Ohrid, Lake Bohinj and the Albanian Riviera; treat kosher, rabbi, chuppah and final pricing as verification items unless a source explicitly confirms them.

3

Lock the date and pay the venue deposit

Once catering and your rabbi are both confirmed available for a date at your shortlisted venue, that's the moment to actually book it. It's the hardest decision on this list to undo, so it's worth being last of this "big three", not first.

Meanwhile, these don't wait on anything above

Start the moodboard whenever inspiration strikes

Pin dress silhouettes, flowers, hair and makeup looks and venue moods as you find them, from this app's Style Gallery, your own photos, or Pinterest. Nothing has to be final; it's a place to notice what you keep coming back to.

∞

Work the Checklist for everything else, already in order

It's sequenced by how far out each task typically needs to happen for a kosher destination wedding: international guests need more runway than a local wedding on every front, from save-the-dates to flights. Once the big three above are moving, work it top to bottom and you won't need to re-figure the order yourself.

Who's Coming

How e-vites actually work: there's no way to "send" from inside this app since it doesn't have email-sending built in, no planner app really does that safely. What you actually do is pick an e-vite service (Paperless Post, Greenvelope, Zola, Joy, etc.), then import your guest list into their platform, and they handle the bulk send and RSVP tracking for you. So the useful thing this app can do is give you an email field per guest, plus a one-click export of names and emails, so you can paste that list straight into whichever service you pick instead of retyping everyone by hand.
