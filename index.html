Start here

A destination wedding in Europe, guests flying in from Israel and France, a chuppah, kosher food, and a villa full of people you love for a whole weekend. Here's the shape of the plan.

1

Pick your region, not just a venue

Because of the kosher + chuppah requirement, the region matters more than any single villa. The original shortlist is still there, but Venues & Destinations now also includes hidden Italian lakes, Montenegro, Istria, Provence hamlets, Cilento, Lake Ohrid, Lake Bohinj and the Albanian Riviera. Treat kosher, rabbi, chuppah and final pricing as verification items unless a source explicitly confirms them.

2

Lock catering and your rabbi before the venue deposit

Kosher catering in Europe is done by a small number of traveling companies and needs the longest lead time of anything on this list — for a full kosher wedding some caterers ask for 12–18 months notice. Confirm a kosher caterer and your officiating rabbi can both work at a venue before you put down a deposit on it.

3

Build the "stay together" weekend

Aim for a villa or masseria with enough bedrooms for your closest guests, plus a walkable cluster of budget hotels or Airbnbs for the overflow — so nobody needs a car to get to the pool party. Capacity notes for exactly this are on each venue card.

4

Start the moodboard now, decide later

Pin dress silhouettes, flowers, hair and makeup looks and venue moods as you find them — from this app's style gallery, from your own photos, or from Pinterest. Nothing has to be final; it's a place to notice what you keep coming back to.

5

Work the checklist top to bottom

It's ordered by how far out each task typically needs to happen for a kosher destination wedding — international guests need more runway than a local wedding on every front, from save-the-dates to flights.

Adding images from Pinterest, Google, or anywhere else: this board can't stay live-connected to any site or hotlink images from one — that's a security boundary of this kind of app, not something I can switch off. Three ways in that actually work: (1) drag an image straight from a Pinterest board, Google Images, or any webpage onto this page (drop it anywhere below) — most browsers hand over the real picture, not just a link. (3) save it to your device first and upload it like any other photo. Only pasting or typing a plain link gets you a clickable reference card rather than the picture itself — still useful, just different.
